Overview

Led a Lean Systems Engineering case study focused on improving material and information flow for an active fireplace mantle production line. The project began with an on-site assessment of a recently launched manufacturing process experiencing delays caused by batching, waiting, and inconsistent flow.

Our team documented the current-state production system, identified sources of waste, and developed a target-state design to improve flow, reduce lead time, and strengthen production control. Deliverables included current- and target-state MIFCs, an A3 report, and a formal presentation of recommendations.

As team lead for eight students, I coordinated project execution, organized fieldwork, and led the development of the target-state MIFC, technical documentation, and final presentation delivered to faculty and industry representatives.

Problem / Goals / Requirements

Problem

  • Excessive waiting and batch processing increased lead time.
  • Limited visibility into work-in-process reduced production flow.
  • Process imbalance created recurring bottlenecks and idle time.

Goals

  • Accurately document the existing production system.
  • Design a leaner target-state process with improved material flow.
  • Reduce lead time and eliminate non-value-added activities.

Requirements

  • Apply standard lean engineering methods including MIFC and A3.
  • Develop practical recommendations within existing production constraints.
  • Present findings clearly to both technical and non-technical audiences.

System / Architecture

The production system consisted of cutting, assembly, finishing, drying, packing, and shipping operations connected through batch-based material flow. Large transfer batches created excess waiting, work-in-process inventory, and limited production visibility.

The proposed target-state introduced FIFO lanes, reduced batch sizes, defined WIP limits, and standardized handoffs to improve material flow while remaining compatible with existing equipment, staffing, and facility constraints.

Execution

  • Conducted on-site observations and time studies to document production flow.
  • Identified bottlenecks and recurring sources of delay.
  • Developed a current-state MIFC representing the existing manufacturing process.

Phase 2 — Target-State Design

  • Analyzed sources of waste across the current production system.
  • Developed a future-state MIFC incorporating FIFO flow, smaller lot sizes, and WIP limits.
  • Defined practical countermeasures to improve production performance.

Phase 3 — Lean Documentation

  • Created a comprehensive A3 summarizing problem definition and root-cause analysis.
  • Documented proposed countermeasures and implementation strategy.
  • Summarized projected performance improvements and practical constraints.

Phase 4 — Presentation & Review

  • Delivered a concise action plan outlining sequencing and follow-up recommendations.

Results & Outcomes

  • Designed a target-state production system projected to reduce total lead time by 66%.
  • Identified improvements estimated to reduce queue-related waiting by approximately 72%.
  • Addressed 2 of the 3 primary production bottlenecks through flow and WIP-control improvements.
  • Identified drying time as the next primary improvement opportunity.
